Totsuka's 95

Published 18th November 2026 Interactive ~4 min read

In February, Mo Wootten and I drew Yuto Totsuka's winning halfpipe run in Tableau. Milano Cortina 2026, run two. Five tricks, 6,120 degrees, a score of 95.00. That workbook is still on the portfolio as Totsuka's 95 | Feb Collab.

I kept wondering if the same piece could be a data app. Keep the layout and the colours. Keep the pipe. Spend the effort on the feel: a path that draws itself in, a rider you can send from the start, and a snowboarder who plays the corks, the spin and the grab.

The page below is that rebuild. Hover a number, or press play, and he rides the hit while the corks, the spin and the grab explain themselves.

The five hits are the February ones: a cab 1440 triple cork with a drunk driver, then a frontside 1440 triple cork with a truck driver. A switch backside alley-oop rodeo 900, a switch backside 1080 double cork, and a backside 1260 double cork to close. Mute is the last grab.
Same pipe as the February viz. Hover a number, or press Ride the run. Scroll the page to stay with him. Open full screen

How I did it

I used Claude to build the page, with the February viz open beside it. The brief was to stay close: pale ice, the blue, the pipe drawing, the dashed path, the score sitting up on the right. The path, the trick codes and the glossary are the collab dataset. The new work is how you move through them.

It is one HTML file, totsukas-95.html. The pipe, the path and the glossary all sit inside it. The same file is the frame above, and the page that opens full screen.

The path is 200 points from that dataset. They are scaled and shifted so they sit on the pipe image, then stretched below the last hit so the finish meets the floor of the pipe. A Catmull-Rom curve joins them, and that curve is the dashed line. On load the grey line draws itself in, and the five dots pop on as the line reaches them.

The rider on the pipe follows the line. The spin and the corks play in the card, where there is room to watch them. He moves through five poses: crouch, pop, tuck, grab, land. Corks tumble him and he comes out upright. Spins turn him edge-on. The cork count lights up underneath, and the chips along the bottom are the trick code, each one still carrying the glossary line from the February workbook. Mute had no definition in the source, so that chip uses a short fallback.

Press play and he leaves the start. The blue dashes follow him. At each dot he waits while the card plays that trick, and the rotation and cork totals count up under the button. At the finish the score eases up to 95.00 and the six judges appear. The highest and the lowest are crossed out. What remains is 95.00. If reduced motion is on, the snow stops and the line draws in at once.

The file reports its height to this article, so the frame grows with the pipe. Opened on its own, the same page follows the rider down as he rides.

The Last Thread

The February version showed the run could be drawn. This one was about whether it could be ridden. The colours stayed put. The part I cared about was him waiting at each hit until the trick had actually happened.
