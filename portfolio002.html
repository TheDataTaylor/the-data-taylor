73 Years. 34 Losses. One Roar.

Published 15th November 2026 Interactive ~4 min read

Wales had not beaten New Zealand since 19 December 1953. On 14 November 2026 they did, 35–28 in Cardiff. That is 26,628 days, and 34 defeats in between. A sentence can hold the number. It cannot hold the wait.

So the piece below is four screens you move through. The headline. A clock that runs from that December in Cardiff to this November, filling a year at a time. Every Test in between, drawn by how much it was won by. Then the Saturday it ended.

The gap: two Welsh wins in the fixture since 1953. The closest defeats were by a single point, in 1978 and again in 2004. The widest was 52 points, 55–3 in Hamilton in 2003.

How I did it

This one is not a Tableau workbook, and it is not a dashboard you filter. It is a short story with four full-height screens, and there is no library in it. The screens snap as you scroll, and each one starts its own animation when it comes into view.

The results are one list. Date, venue, Wales's score, New Zealand's score, and the occasion: a tour, a World Cup, an autumn Test. A win is just Wales scoring more. From that list the page gets the day count, the defeat streak, the margins, and which years to colour. Full Tests only, Wales named first. The 2026 score is the one reported on the day. The earlier ones are the Wales–New Zealand Test history, checked against the match reports.

The first screen counts 26,628 up from zero. The second plays the years. I placed the captions by hand, on the beats worth stopping for: the one-point games, the 1988 tour, Hamilton, Tom Rogers' hat-trick in 2025, and then Cardiff. The clock pauses on each of those. A year turns red for a defeat and white for a win. The background drifts from green toward black as the wait gets longer, and snaps to red when the 2026 result lands. You can pause it, step a Test at a time, tap a year, or drag along the chain of defeats.

The third screen is the same list as bars. The height is the winning margin. Black bars are the games decided by three points or fewer. The two green bars are the Welsh wins. They grow in order, and the chips underneath jump you to the closest loss, the biggest, and the one that ended it.

The last screen is the Saturday. A calendar for November 2026, weekday names in Welsh, the 14th circled, and then the score. If reduced motion is on, the count-up, the year play and the confetti are skipped and the finished state is shown instead.

The Last Thread

Thirty-four defeats is a number you can put in a headline. The useful bit was making someone sit through the years before the score arrived. The roar only lands if the clock has been running.
